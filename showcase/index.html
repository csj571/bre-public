How to read this

The detector is Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection (Adams & MacKay, 2007). At every step it maintains a posterior over run length — how long the current regime has lasted — and a Normal-Inverse-Gamma conjugate model of that regime's mean and variance. When new observations stop looking like the current regime, mass collapses off the growing run and onto run length zero. The middle panel is that collapse; the bottom panel is the mass landing on zero.

Latency is measured in trading days from the pre-registered onset to the first flag. Those onsets — Lehman 2008-09-15, COVID 2020-02-20 — live in validation/markets/validate_regimes.py and were fixed before any series was scored.

Watch the middle panel, not the bottom one. The run-length posterior is the principled output; the discrete flag is a heuristic layer on top of it, and it fires on either of two conditions — P(change) crossing 0.5, or the run-length mode collapsing after a long run. On daily market data it is almost always the second. That is why the bottom panel's axis is narrow: P(change) mostly sits on its hazard prior of 1/λ = 0.02 and rises only slightly, so the axis prints its true range rather than a flattering one. If you build on this, consume the posterior — not the flag stream.

This is detection, not prediction. Every flag lands on or after the onset. Nothing here forecasts a crash, and nothing here is a trading signal — the measurement is how fast an uncertainty-aware model notices that the world changed underneath it.

The negative control matters as much as the hits. Fed a smoothed, strongly autocorrelated series (21-day realized volatility), the same detector misses COVID entirely. That is the documented failure mode of vanilla Adams-MacKay on autocorrelated financial series — an implementation that "detected" everything you fed it would be the suspicious one.

On false alarms, honestly. Over 13.6 years the VIX run produces 14 flags for a 2-break truth set, so the raw false-alarm rate is 0.86. Each extra flag lands on a documented, dateable stress event (Fukushima, the 2015 China devaluation, Volmageddon, …), which is why the harness also reports a documented-adjusted rate of 0.00 — but that secondary event list was compiled after looking at the output, so the pre-registered part of the result is the latency, not the false-alarm rate. The robustness suite goes further: on calm periods the detector draws 5–6 flags a year, and a shuffled placebo fires more often than real data. The supported claim is fast detection of calm→turbulent transitions, not low-false-alarm event detection. Full write-ups: regime_validation.md and robustness_checks.md.
